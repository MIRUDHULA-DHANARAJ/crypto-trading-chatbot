# Technical Analysis

## What is technical analysis?

Technical analysis is the study of market data, primarily price and volume, to identify patterns, trends, momentum, and potential areas of interest.

Technical analysis does not guarantee future price movements.

It is commonly used together with risk management and other forms of market analysis.

## Candlestick charts

Candlestick charts represent price movement during a specific period.

Each candle typically contains four values:

- Open
- High
- Low
- Close

The open is the price at the beginning of the period.

The close is the price at the end of the period.

The high is the highest traded price during the period.

The low is the lowest traded price during the period.

## Candle body

The candle body represents the difference between the opening and closing prices.

If the close is above the open, the candle represents an upward price movement for that period.

If the close is below the open, it represents a downward price movement.

## Candle wicks

The upper wick represents the difference between the highest price and the upper part of the candle body.

The lower wick represents the difference between the lowest price and the lower part of the candle body.

Wicks can provide information about price rejection during a period.

## Doji

A Doji is a candlestick in which the opening and closing prices are relatively close.

A Doji can indicate uncertainty or a temporary balance between buying and selling pressure.

Its meaning depends heavily on the surrounding market structure.

A Doji does not guarantee a reversal.

## Hammer

A Hammer is generally characterized by a relatively small body and a long lower wick.

It commonly appears after a decline.

The pattern can indicate rejection of lower prices.

Confirmation from subsequent price action can be important because a Hammer alone does not guarantee a bullish reversal.

## Shooting star

A Shooting Star generally has a relatively small body and a long upper wick.

It can appear after an upward movement.

The pattern may indicate rejection of higher prices.

A Shooting Star is not a guaranteed bearish reversal signal.

## Bullish engulfing

A bullish engulfing pattern generally consists of a smaller bearish candle followed by a larger bullish candle whose body encompasses the previous candle's body.

It can indicate increased buying pressure.

Its significance depends on factors such as market trend, location, volume, and subsequent price behavior.

## Bearish engulfing

A bearish engulfing pattern generally consists of a smaller bullish candle followed by a larger bearish candle whose body encompasses the previous candle's body.

It can indicate increased selling pressure.

It should be interpreted in the context of the broader market structure.

## Support

Support is a price area where buying interest has previously slowed or stopped declines.

Support can form around previous lows, consolidation areas, moving averages, or other technically significant levels.

Support can fail when selling pressure becomes sufficiently strong.

## Resistance

Resistance is a price area where selling interest has previously slowed or stopped advances.

Resistance can form around previous highs, consolidation areas, moving averages, or other technical levels.

Resistance can be broken when buying pressure becomes sufficiently strong.

## Trend lines

A trend line is a line drawn across selected price points to represent a directional movement.

An upward trend line can connect higher lows.

A downward trend line can connect lower highs.

Trend lines are subjective and different traders may draw them differently.

## Moving average

A moving average smooths price data over a specified number of periods.

Moving averages can be used to identify trends, dynamic support or resistance, and changes in momentum.

They are lagging indicators because they are calculated from historical prices.

## Simple Moving Average

A Simple Moving Average, or SMA, calculates the arithmetic mean of prices over a specified number of periods.

For example, a 20-period SMA is calculated by adding the relevant 20 prices and dividing by 20.

The oldest observation is removed as a new observation enters the calculation.

## Exponential Moving Average

An Exponential Moving Average, or EMA, gives greater weight to recent prices.

As a result, an EMA typically responds more quickly to recent price changes than an SMA with the same period.

EMAs are commonly used in trend and momentum analysis.

## Moving average crossover

A moving average crossover occurs when one moving average crosses another.

For example, a shorter-period moving average crossing above a longer-period moving average may be interpreted by some traders as a bullish signal.

Crossovers can produce false signals, particularly in sideways markets.

## Relative Strength Index

The Relative Strength Index, or RSI, is a momentum oscillator that commonly uses a 14-period calculation.

RSI values range from 0 to 100.

Traditional interpretations often consider readings above 70 as potentially overbought and readings below 30 as potentially oversold.

These levels do not guarantee that price will reverse.

## RSI divergence

RSI divergence occurs when price movement and RSI movement show different directional behavior.

For example, price may form a higher high while RSI forms a lower high.

Traders sometimes interpret divergence as a possible indication of changing momentum.

Divergence is not a guaranteed reversal signal.

## MACD

Moving Average Convergence Divergence, or MACD, is a momentum and trend-following indicator based on exponential moving averages.

The standard MACD calculation uses a fast EMA, a slow EMA, and a signal line.

A histogram is commonly used to visualize the difference between the MACD line and signal line.

## MACD crossover

A MACD crossover occurs when the MACD line crosses the signal line.

A crossover above the signal line may be interpreted as increasing bullish momentum.

A crossover below the signal line may be interpreted as increasing bearish momentum.

Crossovers can produce false signals.

## Bollinger Bands

Bollinger Bands consist of a moving average and upper and lower bands based on a measure of price variability.

The bands generally expand when volatility increases and contract when volatility decreases.

Prices moving near or beyond a band do not automatically indicate that a reversal will occur.

## Bollinger Band squeeze

A Bollinger Band squeeze describes a period in which the bands become relatively narrow.

Some traders interpret a squeeze as a sign that volatility may increase later.

The squeeze itself does not determine the direction of a future price movement.

## VWAP

Volume Weighted Average Price, or VWAP, represents the average traded price weighted by trading volume over a specified period.

VWAP can provide a reference for the relationship between price and volume-weighted market activity.

VWAP is often used by intraday traders.

## Volume analysis

Volume represents the amount of an asset traded during a specified period.

Traders may compare volume with price movements to evaluate the strength of a move.

High volume during a breakout can provide additional context, but it does not guarantee that the breakout will continue.

## Volatility

Volatility describes the magnitude and frequency of price changes.

Higher volatility means prices can move more substantially over a given period.

Volatility is not the same as direction. An asset can have high volatility while moving sideways overall.

## Average True Range

Average True Range, or ATR, is an indicator designed to measure market volatility.

ATR does not directly indicate whether price will rise or fall.

Traders may use ATR to understand typical price movement and to help determine risk-management parameters.

## Fibonacci retracement

Fibonacci retracement levels are horizontal levels derived from ratios associated with the Fibonacci sequence.

Commonly referenced ratios include approximately 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, and 78.6%.

Traders may use these levels as potential areas of support or resistance.

Fibonacci levels are analytical tools and are not guaranteed price targets.

## Breakout

A breakout occurs when price moves beyond a significant support or resistance area.

Breakouts can be confirmed or rejected.

A false breakout occurs when price briefly moves beyond a level but subsequently returns within the previous range.

## False breakout

A false breakout occurs when price moves beyond an important level but fails to sustain the move.

Traders may examine volume, candle closes, retests, and subsequent price action to distinguish potential breakouts from false breakouts.

No confirmation method guarantees success.

## Market structure

Market structure refers to the arrangement of price highs and lows.

Common structures include:

- Higher highs and higher lows
- Lower highs and lower lows
- Ranges
- Consolidation

Market structure can provide context for interpreting indicators and chart patterns.

## Confluence

Confluence refers to multiple independent technical observations supporting a similar interpretation.

For example, a trader may observe support, a moving average, and a momentum signal near the same area.

Confluence does not eliminate uncertainty.

## Limitations of technical analysis

Technical analysis has several limitations:

- Indicators are based on historical data.
- Different traders can interpret the same chart differently.
- Signals can conflict.
- False signals occur.
- Market conditions can change.
- Extreme events can invalidate technical setups.
- No indicator guarantees future price direction.

Technical analysis should therefore be treated as an analytical framework rather than a prediction engine.
